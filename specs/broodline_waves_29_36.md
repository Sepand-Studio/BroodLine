*Content, chapter five authored*

**The chapter was authored to test a flagged risk and the risk was real.** `broodline_combat_numbers.md` §6.1 asked whether an unaided Skitter trivialises Bulwark. It does, and the fix is not the shield value — it is what the shield is. See §5.

## 1. Chapter 5 — waves 29 to 36

**Two lanes throughout. Integrity 6. Introduces Bulwark. Milestone 7 at wave 33.**

## 4. Bulwark's slowness is its design

Sixty seconds to cross a lane is by far the longest of any raider — Courser does it in fifteen. Authoring eight waves around it, three properties emerge that are worth stating.

**It gives the player time and charges them for wasting it.** Four integrity of a pool of six is the heaviest cost in the game, and the wave that lets it through had a minute to prevent it.

**It converts other raiders into pressure.** A Courser entering the lane a Sprint carrier is committed to (wave 31) is a decision the same Courser would not force alone. Bulwark's job in a mixed wave is to occupy a creature, not to threaten by itself.

**Two Bulwarks in two lanes is a genuinely different problem from one**, more so than for any other raider, because a single Sprint carrier cannot be in both lanes for a minute each. That is what makes wave 32's coverage lesson land harder than wave 24's.

## 5. The shield lock was soft, and the fix is structural

`broodline_combat_numbers.md` §6.1 flagged it: *"400 shield at 25 per hit is 16 hits. Skitter at 0.4s does that in 6.4 seconds unaided, which makes Sprint look optional."* The document proposed raising the shield to 600 if playtest confirmed it.

**Authoring wave 30 confirms it, and 600 is the wrong fix.**

At 25 per hit regardless of damage, an unaided Skitter strips a 400 shield in 6.4 seconds and kills the 300 HP body in eleven — seventeen seconds of a sixty-second crossing, with no Sprint anywhere. Raising the shield to 600 makes that 9.6 seconds instead of 6.4. It does not change the answer.

**The deeper problem is that Sprint is the only counter whose species answers the raider without carrying the trait.** Chill on a Pale that lacks Chill does nothing. Sprint's mechanic *is* Skitter's base attack rate, so a Skitter answers Bulwark whether or not it carries the trait it is supposed to need. The lock is soft in a way the design does not intend, and no shield value fixes that.

> **Revised: the shield blocks damage entirely, and only hits from a creature carrying Sprint degrade it, at 100 per hit.**

- Shield 400. **Damage does not pass it.** It is not an HP pool
- **It degrades only from Sprint hits**, at 100 each — four hits to break, on any body
- **Coverage tier sets how many Bulwarks a carrier can be stripping at once**: I one, II two, III three

**This makes Bulwark and Breaker the matched pair their shared body already implies.** Breaker's Plate caps incoming damage and Pierce lifts the cap; Bulwark's Shield blocks it outright and Sprint strips the shield. Two variants of the Hauler, two variants of the same lesson, and both scale by simultaneity like every other counter except Cinder and Reach.

**Sprint now works on any body, which matters.** Traits are bred onto species, and a Sprint carrier that had to be a Skitter would make the trait a species tax. At 100 per hit, a Skitter at 0.4s breaks a shield in 1.6 seconds and an Ember at 1.7s in 6.8 — both fine, both worth doing.

**This is a change to `broodline_combat_numbers.md` §4.2 and §6.** It is the second time authoring has found a lock that was not one; the first was Breaker's Plate, found by arithmetic rather than by authoring. **The remaining raider worth re-checking on the same basis is Delver**, whose submersion is answered by Burrow at chapter 6 — a hard binary that looks sound, and looked sound for Bulwark too.

## 6. The four-species case stays authored

`broodline_waves_21_28.md` §5 authored the four-species worst case at wave 27 and said it should not recur before chapter 6.

**Holding that through chapter 5 took deliberate composition**, because Bulwark makes it easy to hit by accident. Skitter carries only Sprint, so any four-type wave including Bulwark and three raiders from three other species scatters across four — and with Bulwark in seven of eight waves, that combination is the default rather than the exception.

**The rule that avoids it: include both raiders from one species' pair.** Ember carries Splash and Cinder, so any wave with both Skirmishers and Brood spends two raider types on one species. Hollow carries Reach and Pierce, which will do the same job from chapter 7. Every four-type wave in this chapter uses the Ember pairing, and none exceeds three species.

**This should be a standing check on every authored wave**: count species, not types. Four types is the stated cap and it is the easy one; four *species* is the one that actually reaches the deployment limit, and nothing currently flags it.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Does Bulwark's revised shield make wave 30 too easy for a player who happens to hold Sprint?** Four hits from a Skitter is 1.6 seconds. The introduction wave may now be trivial in the opposite direction, and the discount at 61% assumed the old maths.
2. **Four integrity for a Bulwark against a pool of six is untested.** It makes a single leak nearly fatal from wave 30 onward, which is either the right weight for the slowest raider in the game or a difficulty spike hidden in a cost table.
3. **Wave 35's 1.3-second Skirmisher interval** is the second wave to run faster than the designed 1.5. Both should be reverted if the interval test at wave 10 shows 1.5 is already at the edge.
4. **Chapter 5 has no wave requiring a utility trait**, and neither did chapters 1 to 4. Thirty-six waves without Carapace, Litter, Regrow or Screen being asked for. That remains correct — they are choices — but the design's largest open risk will be forty-five waves old before anything tests it.
