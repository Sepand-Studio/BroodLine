# Broodline — Campaign Waves 45–52

*Content, chapter seven authored*

> **CURRENT — content, not design.** Schema and conventions in
> `broodline_waves_01_12.md` §1. This document is chapter 7.

**Two findings.** Wave 50 is the first wave in the campaign that requires a utility trait, and it gets there structurally rather than by fiat — §5. And **the composition ceiling is reached at wave 51**, which means chapter 8 has no escalation room left in what a wave contains — §6.

---

## 1. Chapter 7 — waves 45 to 52

**Three lanes throughout. Integrity 8. Introduces Breaker. Milestones 9 at wave 48 and 10 at wave 52.**

**Authored against the four-type cap, with budget checked afterward**, per `broodline_waves_37_44.md` §6. Every wave here sits between 51% and 73% of formula budget and none of them could legally go higher.

### Wave 46 — Breaker

| | |
|---|---|
| Lanes | **3 · Scree**, converging for the last 6 tiles, 5 pockets |
| Integrity | 8 |
| Composition | **1 Breaker · 2 Brood · 2 Lash · 45 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | Lash t=3, lanes A and C. Skirmishers from t=4, 1.5s. Brood t=8, A and B. **Breaker t=5, lane B** |
| Budget | 895 of 1,752. **51% — the hardest lock in the game** |
| Teaches | **Armour that damage does not get through** |
| Expected roster | 5, Hollow carrying Pierce since Founder 1 |
| First clear | 1,380 shards · 3 tier-I samples · 1 Gen-1 creature |

**One Breaker, in the middle lane, on converging terrain, at half the formula budget.**

**Sixty-eight seconds to cross and nothing the player does matters for any of them.** Every incoming hit caps at 5 damage against 600 HP that regenerates 15 a second — the arithmetic is not close, it is not a grind, it simply does not work. A player without Pierce watches an animal walk the length of the lane while five creatures shoot at it.

**Taught last, and deliberately.** `broodline_campaign_structure.md` §2.1: Breaker is the most punishing raider to meet without its answer, and Hollow is Founder 1, so by wave 46 the player has carried Pierce for six weeks and only needs to work out that this is what it was for. Meeting it at wave 6 would have been the counter lesson taught as a brick wall.

**A Scree, not the Weir the previous wave used.** Converging lanes give a single Pierce carrier near the merge a chance to matter in more than one of them.

**Two Breakers at 4 integrity each against a pool of 8.** Both through is exactly a loss. Pierce I stops one and the player survives on 4; Pierce II stops both.

Fifth chapter running with the same rhythm — introduce a counter, two waves later show its tier. Chill at 12, Cinder at 18, Reach at 24, Sprint at 32, Burrow at 41, Pierce at 48.

**Two Breakers in the outer lanes, not adjacent.** On a Scree they converge late, so a Pierce carrier at the merge can reach both — but only one at a time at tier I. The geometry says the answer is available; the coverage says it is not enough.

**The hardest terrain in the game, introduced with nothing new in it.** Every raider here was met before wave 20.

**Delta is the geometry the whole design has been building toward.** Three parallel lanes and four pockets means five creatures cannot cover everything and there is no merge point to cheat with. `broodline_region_roster.md` §4's inverse rule puts Delta in the richest Outer Reach regions, so a player who wants the best nodes fights on this ground — and this is the wave that teaches them what that costs.

**No Breaker, no Bulwark, no Delver, no Drift.** Introducing the hardest terrain alongside a hard lock would test two things at once and teach neither.

### Wave 50 — Hollow, twice over

| | |
|---|---|
| Lanes | 3 · Delta |
| Integrity | 8 |
| Composition | **2 Breakers · 3 Drifts · 2 Lash · 50 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | Skirmishers from t=3, 1.5s. **Lash t=5, lanes A and C.** Breakers t=6, A and C. **Drifts t=11, one per lane** |

| Expected roster | 5, and it wants a Screen carrier |
| First clear | 1,540 shards · 3 tier-I samples · 1 Gen-1 creature |

**Two species, four raider types.** Breaker and Drift both want Hollow — Pierce for the armour, Reach for the sky — and a Hollow can carry both, since a creature has two combat trait slots.

**Which puts the whole wave on a 60 HP body.** Hollow is the frailest creature in the game and this wave asks it to answer two of the four threats on three parallel lanes.

**And then two Lash, which target the furthest defender in range.** That is the Hollow, at range 7, standing where it can cover the most sky. The wave is constructed so that the raider most able to kill the linchpin is aimed at it by its own targeting rule.

**This is the first wave in the campaign that a utility trait genuinely answers.** See §5.

## 4. Breaker is taught last and it shows

Six weeks of carrying Pierce before meeting the thing it answers is a long time, and authoring wave 46 makes the case for it concrete.

**The Codex has to have been working.** A player who has never opened it meets an animal that ignores everything they do, and the only reason that reads as a puzzle rather than a bug is that they have been told twice already — at wave 6 and wave 22 — that raiders have answers. Breaker is the payoff for a lesson taught twice.

**Hollow has been the answer three times now.** Founder 1, Reach for Drift at wave 22, Pierce for Breaker at wave 46. Whichever species is Founder 1 accumulates that weight, and Hollow was chosen for it — `broodline_base_stock.md` §5.1 picked it because a player should still be fielding their named Founder in month three. Wave 50 is where that decision is cashed.

**It also makes the Sunder legible.** Chapter 8's capstone is a Hauler carrying both Plate and Shield, answered by Pierce and Sprint together. A player who met Breaker at 46 and fought both variants side by side at 47 will read the Sunder correctly on sight.

## 5. Wave 50 is the first wave a utility trait answers

Forty-nine waves have passed without Carapace, Litter, Regrow or Screen being required by anything. That was correct — they are choices, not requirements — but it left the design's largest open risk untested for most of the campaign.

**Wave 50 tests it, and it gets there structurally rather than by fiat.**

Nothing about the wave says "bring Screen." What it says is: Breaker and Drift both want Hollow, a Hollow can carry both Pierce and Reach in its two combat slots, that puts three lanes of sky and two armoured raiders on a single 60 HP body — and there are two Lash, whose targeting rule sends them at the furthest defender in range, which is the Hollow standing far enough back to cover the sky.

**Four answers exist and each is a different trade:**

| Answer | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Screen** on a Pale beside the Hollow | −45% incoming at tier II, and it intercepts Lash. One slot, and the Pale is doing nothing else |
| **Taunt** on a Vetch | Pulls the Lash off, but Taunt I holds one and there are two |
| **A second Hollow** | Splits the load, costs a slot, and both are still 60 HP |
| **Carapace** on the Hollow itself | Wrong. It occupies the slot Pierce or Reach needs |

**Screen is the best of them and it is not obviously the best**, which is what a good test looks like. A player who solves wave 50 with Screen has discovered that a trait countering nothing is the reason their counters survived.

**This should be instrumented ahead of everything except the Litter question.** `broodline_build_order.md` §5 lists trait utility balance as the first thing playtest has to answer, and until now no wave asked it. If Screen is not chosen at wave 50, it is not going to be chosen anywhere.

## 6. The composition ceiling is reached at wave 51

`broodline_waves_37_44.md` §6 found that the budget formula stops binding at three lanes because the four-type cap binds first. Chapter 7 reaches that ceiling.

**Wave 51 at 1,565 is the highest-spend wave in the campaign, and nothing later can exceed it by composition.** The constraints are hard:

- Four raider types maximum
- Three of a type is the practical maximum, because coverage tiers answer three
- Skirmishers are the only unbounded filler, and sixty is already the largest count authored

Three each of the three most expensive raiders — Breaker at 145, Bulwark at 120, Brood at 90 — plus sixty Skirmishers is **1,665**, and that is the arithmetic ceiling of a legal wave. Wave 51 is at 94% of it.

**So chapter 8 cannot escalate by composition.** Eight waves after the heaviest possible wave, with the same eight raiders, the same cap, the same tiers. Three dials remain:

| Dial | What it buys |
|---|---|
| **Integrity** | Chapter 8 runs at 10, so more gets through before the pool empties — but it also makes leaks cheaper, which cuts the wrong way |
| **Spawn density and ordering** | Everything simultaneous rather than staggered. Real, and unmeasured by the budget |
| **The Sunder** | A raider that costs more per body than anything else and demands two counters at once |

**The Sunder is not a flourish, then. It is chapter 8's only source of escalation**, and it is worth noting that it was designed before this was known — `broodline_raider_roster.md` §7 justified it as a capstone that cannot be beaten by stacking one species. It turns out to also be the only way chapter 8 can be harder than chapter 7.

**Which means chapter 8 should be authored around ordering and timing rather than contents**, and the eight waves should read as variations on a fixed set rather than as escalation. That is a different authoring problem from the previous seven chapters and it should be approached as one.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Wave 50 is the single most important wave to instrument.** It is the only test of whether a utility trait is ever worth a slot, and if it fails there is no second chance in the campaign.
2. **Is one Breaker at wave 46 too thin on three lanes?** At 51% of budget it is the lowest-spend introduction since the tutorial. The argument is that Breaker unanswered is a guaranteed 4 integrity and there is no partial outcome; the counter-argument is that two lanes of routine content around it may make the lesson easy to miss.
3. **Delta at wave 49 with four pockets and five creatures is untested as a feeling.** It may be the wave where the deployment cap stops feeling tight and starts feeling insufficient.
4. **Sixty Skirmishers plus three Broods becoming thirty-nine is close to a hundred entities.** Flagged in chapter 6 as a performance question and it recurs at wave 52.
5. **Chapter 8 needs a different authoring approach and does not have one yet.** Ordering, timing and integrity are the only dials left, and none of them has a formula.
