# Broodline — Seasonal Chapters

*Content spec, how to author waves 61 onward*

> **CURRENT — content spec.** `broodline_waves_53_60.md` §7 flags that seasonal
> chapters inherit chapter 8's problem and its method, and leaves the rest open.
> This is the rest.

**The finding that shapes everything else: seasonal chapters cannot continue the campaign's escalation, so they should not continue its numbering** — §2. They are a parallel track at fixed difficulty, differentiated by shape and terrain rather than by being harder.

---

## 1. What a seasonal chapter is for

Bible §8.3 runs four-week seasons. `broodline_campaign_structure.md` §10 continues the campaign into them with **Core capped at 12** — so seasonal waves grant cosmetics, shards, samples, base stock and Calibration materials, never new facility depth.

**Which means seasonal waves have no progression to gate.** The campaign's job was to gate Core tiers behind proof of engagement. That job is finished at wave 60, and a seasonal chapter that pretends otherwise is inventing a ladder with nothing at the top.

> **A seasonal chapter's job is to be a new problem, not a longer one.**

---

## 2. The numbering has to stop

The campaign runs 1 to 60 on a rising budget and a rising lane count. **Seasonal chapters cannot continue either.**

**The budget formula stopped binding at wave 40.** `broodline_waves_45_52.md` §6 puts the arithmetic ceiling of a legal three-lane wave at 1,665 points against a wave-60 budget near 3,050. Continuing the curve to wave 268 after two years of seasons produces budgets in the tens of thousands describing waves that top out at 1,665.

**And there is no third dial.** Three lanes is the maximum, four raider types is the cap, coverage tiers answer three, and `broodline_raider_roster.md` §7 permits exactly one dual-lock raider which already exists at wave 60.

> **Seasonal chapters are numbered separately — Season 1 Chapter A, waves A1–A8 — at a fixed difficulty near wave 55.**

**Near wave 55 rather than wave 60**, deliberately. Wave 60 is the campaign's capstone and commits all five deployment slots; a season that opened at that intensity every four weeks would make the capstone ordinary.

---

## 3. Eight waves per season

Matches the campaign's chapter length and fits a four-week season without needing to be rationed.

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | **8 waves** |
| Lanes | 3, throughout |
| Integrity | 8 to 10, authored per wave |
| Difficulty | Fixed near wave 55. **No rise across the chapter** except the closing wave |
| Rewards | Shards, samples, base stock, cosmetics, Calibration materials |
| Milestones | **None.** Core is capped |

**The closing wave is the only one that escalates**, and it escalates by shape rather than by contents — the same move chapter 8's wave 60 makes without the Sunder.

---

## 4. The shape vocabulary

Chapter 8 established eight shapes. **They are a vocabulary, not a list to reuse**, and each season should draw a different four or five from a growing set.

**Used in chapter 8:**

| Shape | |
|---|---|
| **All at once** | Everything inside three seconds |
| **Sequential** | Four blocks, nothing overlapping |
| **Asymmetric** | Everything heavy in one lane, harassment in the others |
| **Mirror** | Three identical lanes |
| **Late reveal** | Twenty seconds of nothing, then everything |
| **Rehearsal** | A composition that demands two counters simultaneously |
| **Untouchable** | Breaker, Delver and Drift together |
| **Capstone** | Maximum species requirement |

**Available and unused:**

| Shape | |
|---|---|
| **Reverse ramp** | Heaviest first, thinning across the wave. Punishes a player who holds their Rally |
| **Single lane** | Three lanes, threat in one. Which one is not shown until it starts |
| **No filler** | Zero Skirmishers. Every body on the board is a threat with an answer |
| **Alternating** | Lanes trade off in pulses, so no placement is right for long |
| **Convergent** | A Scree where all three lanes' threats meet at the merge simultaneously |
| **Slow** | Only Breakers and Bulwarks. Ninety seconds of almost nothing moving |
| **Fast** | Only Coursers, Skirmishers and Drifts. Twenty-five seconds end to end |
| **Split integrity** | A low pool with cheap raiders, or a high pool with expensive ones |

**Slow and Fast are the two most distinctive and neither has been used.** A wave made only of Breakers and Bulwarks lasts ninety seconds with four bodies on the board; a wave of Coursers and Drifts is over in twenty-five. Both are legal, both feel unlike anything in the campaign, and both are free.

---

## 5. Terrain is the season's identity

`broodline_region_roster.md` §3 has eight terrain families and the campaign uses all of them across sixty waves.

> **A season picks one family and stays on it.**

Eight families, so **eight seasons before a repeat** — two years of visual and tactical identity out of content that already exists.

**A Weir season is about damage rate.** `broodline_region_roster.md` §3 makes it the only family whose difficulty is about killing fast enough rather than about counter breadth, so a season on it rewards a completely different roster from a Delta season.

**A Defile season is single-lane at wave-55 intensity**, which the campaign never does — one lane and six pockets carrying the threat of a three-lane wave. That is a genuinely new problem and it costs nothing to author.

**The terrain is announced with the season.** A player should be able to breed toward it, which is the whole point of a four-week cycle with a known shape.

---

## 6. What a season may never do

Carried from `broodline_campaign_structure.md` §10 and `broodline_raider_roster.md` §7, and restated because seasonal content is where these break.

- **No seasonal wave requires seasonal content.** No species, trait or Aberrant introduced this season may be needed to clear this season's waves. That converts the Season Pass into a progression requirement and makes a lapsed month permanently costly
- **No second dual-lock raider.** Exactly one exists, at campaign wave 60
- **No raider gains stats.** Escalation is by count, lane, integrity and shape. Never by making a Breaker tougher
- **No Core progression.** Capped at 12
- **No new counter without a new silhouette.** See §7

---

## 7. When a new raider is worth it

The tempting answer to running out of escalation is a ninth raider. **It is a season-scale production decision, not an authoring one.**

`broodline_raider_roster.md` §10: a new lock needs a new silhouette, and a new silhouette is a new body — because the whole recognition model rests on the mechanic being readable off the shape.

**A ninth raider costs:**

| | |
|---|---|
| A new body and two variant kits | 3 assets, against a total of 36 |
| Locomotion, ability and death animations | ~5 clips |
| A ninth counter trait, which is a **thirteenth trait** | Breaks the twelve-trait Codex and the six-species pairing |

**That last one is the real cost and it is structural.** Bible §1.2 puts twelve traits across six species, two each, with eight counters and four utility. A ninth counter means either a thirteenth trait on an existing species — breaking the two-each symmetry — or a **seventh species**, which is a body, two traits, an Instinct signature, a colour, ten weighted regions, and a re-audit of the deployment cap.

> **A ninth raider is a seventh species. Price it that way.**

**Which is fine, once.** A seventh species as a major season is a real expansion and it is the correct shape for one. What it is not is the answer to a season needing to feel different, and the shape vocabulary at §4 exists so it is never mistaken for one.

---

## 8. Authoring checklist

For each seasonal wave, in order:

1. **Pick a shape** from §4. Do not reuse one within a season
2. **Compose against the four-type cap**, not against a budget. Budget is a sanity check
3. **Count species, not types** — `broodline_waves_29_36.md` §6. Four species is a deliberate rarity; five is the campaign capstone and belongs nowhere else
4. **Check the no-two-same-answer rule.** Never two raiders answered by the same trait
5. **Author integrity** to make the shape's threat weight land
6. **Check nothing seasonal is required** to clear it
7. **Check spend** against the 1,665 ceiling. Under it, always

---

## 9. Guardrails

- Seasonal chapters are numbered separately from the campaign
- Difficulty is fixed near wave 55 and does not rise across a chapter or across seasons
- Eight waves, three lanes, one terrain family per season
- No shape reused within a season
- No milestones, no Core progression
- No seasonal wave requires seasonal content
- One dual-lock raider exists, at campaign wave 60, and there is never a second
- A ninth raider is a seventh species and is priced as an expansion

---

## 10. Open questions

1. **Fixed difficulty across two years of seasons is the right call and it will be questioned.** A player at season twelve is far stronger than one at season one, and the waves are the same. The answer is that Core is capped, coverage tops out at tier III and generation stops mattering past G6 — so a player is not far stronger, only better equipped. That holds only if those caps hold.
2. **Eight seasons before a terrain repeats is two years.** Whether a repeated family reads as a return or as a shortage depends on how distinctive the first pass made it.
3. **Nothing here says what a season's waves are worth.** Rewards are listed as shards, samples, base stock and cosmetics; the amounts belong in `broodline_economy_model.md` and are unset.
4. **Slow and Fast at §4 are the two shapes most likely to be genuinely new and most likely to fail.** Ninety seconds with four bodies moving may be tension or may be boredom, and twenty-five seconds may be a wave or may be a blink. Both should be prototyped before a season is built on one.
