# Broodline — Campaign Waves 13–20

*Content, chapter three authored*

> **CURRENT — content, not design.** Schema and conventions are set in
> `broodline_waves_01_12.md` §1. This document is chapter 3.

**The question this chapter was authored to answer:** waves 9 to 12 needed 126–162% of formula budget to feel right. If chapter 3 also ran that hot, the growth rate rather than the base was wrong. **It does not.** See §4.

---

## 1. Chapter 3 — waves 13 to 20

**One lane rising to two. Integrity 4 rising to 6. Introduces Brood. Milestones 4 at wave 17 and 5 at wave 20.**

Chapter 3 does two structural things and deliberately does not do them together. **Brood arrives at wave 14 on a single lane**, so the split is legible. **The second lane arrives at wave 16** with nothing new in it. Bible §9.1's one-new-system-per-session rule applies to waves as much as to screens.

### Wave 13

| | |
|---|---|
| Lanes | 1 · Basin, 4 pockets |
| Integrity | 4 |
| Composition | **1 Courser · 1 Lash · 4 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | Lash t=3. Skirmishers from t=5, 1.5s. Courser t=10 |

**A quiet wave before a loud one.** Wave 12 closed chapter 2 on a coverage lesson and wave 14 introduces the most visually dramatic raider in the game. Putting a familiar wave between them is what makes the second one land.

### Wave 14 — Brood

| | |
|---|---|
| Lanes | 1 · Basin |
| Integrity | 4 |
| Composition | **1 Brood · 6 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | Skirmishers from t=3, 1.5s. Brood t=8 |
| Budget | 150 of 167. **90% — deliberately under** |
| Teaches | **A death can create more raiders** |
| Expected roster | 5 |
| First clear | 355 shards · 2 tier-I samples · 1 Gen-1 creature |

**One Brood, and the wave is under budget, because Brood's cost badly under-represents what it puts on the board.** Ninety points buys one body that becomes thirteen — 150 HP, then three at 50, then nine at 20. A player without any Cinder watches one animal become a crowd.

That is the whole lesson and it does not need help. Six Skirmishers alongside keep the lane from being empty; anything more would obscure which thing multiplied.

**Cinder is on Ember, which the player has held since Founder 3.** Like the Vetch holding the Lash at wave 2, this is a counter working before the player knows the word.

### Wave 16 — two lanes

| | |
|---|---|
| Lanes | **2 · Fork**, converging for the last 6 tiles, 5 pockets |
| Integrity | 5 |
| Composition | **1 Courser · 1 Lash · 10 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | Lane A: Skirmishers from t=3, 1.5s. Lane B: Lash t=4, Courser t=11 |
| Budget | 245 of 360. **68% — deliberately far under** |
| Teaches | **Two lanes.** Nothing else |
| Expected roster | 5 |
| First clear | 400 shards · 3 tier-I samples |

**Nothing new arrives except the lane, and the wave is authored at two thirds of budget for it.**

**A Fork, not a Span.** The two lanes converge for the final six tiles, so a Hollow at range 7 placed near the merge contributes to both. That is the gentlest possible introduction to a second lane — the player can solve it with a placement they already understand before they meet a layout where they cannot.

**Doubling the budget on a lane change is the formula's biggest single jump**, and authored waves should almost never spend it. A wave that doubles its threat at the same moment the board changes shape teaches nothing except that the game got harder.

**The first wave in the game to hit the four-type cap**, and it lands on a milestone so the player is paying attention.

**It is also the best case of the audit at `broodline_combat_numbers.md` §8.1.** Four counters wanted — Splash, Cinder, Chill, Taunt — but Ember carries both Splash and Cinder, so three creatures cover all four and two deployment slots stay free. The worst case, where four counters scatter across four species, cannot occur until Bulwark, Delver, Drift or Breaker exist. **Chapter 6 is the earliest it can happen**, and that is worth knowing before it does.

**Cinder I catches the first split generation only.** Two Broods dying unanswered is six Broodlings and eighteen Mites, one lane each. With Cinder I the player gets six bodies instead of twenty-six; with Cinder II, three.

**This is chapter 3's fusing wave**, the same job wave 12 did for Chill. The pattern is deliberate: introduce a counter, then two waves later show the tier.

**The Fork becomes a Span and the wave gets harder without a single extra raider.** Two parallel lanes mean the Hollow that was covering both from near the merge now covers one, and a Chill carrier has to choose a side.

**Two Coursers, simultaneously, one per lane.** Chill I slows one. The other arrives on schedule in a lane the player is not standing in. This is the same lesson as wave 12 delivered through geometry instead of count, which is what makes it a step rather than a repeat.

**Three of the eight waves introduce nothing.** Waves 13, 15 and 20 are consolidation, geometry and climax respectively, and a chapter that introduced something new eight times would be unlearnable. The teaching waves are 14, 16, 17, 18 and 19 — five in eight, which is about the right density and worth holding as a rule for chapters 4 through 8.

## 4. The growth rate is right

The question posed at the end of chapter 2: waves 9–12 ran at 126–162% of budget, and if chapter 3 ran as hot then 1.04 was wrong and should rise to about 1.05.

**Chapter 3 runs at 68–116%, averaging 97% across the six waves that are not the lane change or the milestone.** Budget and spend converge as soon as the chapter has more than three raider types to spend on.

**So the formula was never the problem. The palette was.**

With only Skirmisher, Lash and Courser available, a wave can only reach its budget by stacking counts — and **counts of the same raider do not add threat linearly.** Splash catches two, three or five targets per swing regardless of whether twelve or twenty Skirmishers are on the board; Taunt pins one Lash whether there are two or four. Doubling a raider count well past the coverage tier that answers it adds cost without adding difficulty, so chapters 1 and 2 had to overspend to feel like anything.

**Leave the base at 100 and the growth at 1.04.** Re-check at chapter 5, when Drift and Bulwark widen the palette again and the budget doubles a second time on the third lane.

**The real correction is to how the formula is read.** It is a ceiling on threat, not a target to hit. Waves that introduce something should sit well under it — 68% for the lane change, 90% for Brood — and only consolidation and milestone waves should approach or exceed it. Chapters 1 and 2 were authored as though budget were a target, which is why they look anomalous next to this chapter.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Wave 16 at 68% may still be too much.** It is the largest structural change in the first twenty waves and it could plausibly run at 50% — one lane's worth of threat spread across two lanes, so the player learns the geometry against a load they could already handle.
2. **Wave 20 having no Skirmishers is untested as a signal.** It should read as *this wave is different*; it might just read as *this wave is short*.
3. **The four-species worst case cannot occur before chapter 6.** That is a consequence of the raider order rather than a decision, and it means the deployment-cap pressure the audit worries about goes untested for forty waves. Worth deliberately authoring an early four-species wave in chapter 6 rather than letting it arrive by accident.
4. **Fork before Span is the right order, but Shelf is unused so far.** The staggered family — one lane six tiles shorter — has no home yet in the campaign and probably belongs in chapter 4 or 5, before three lanes make it moot.
