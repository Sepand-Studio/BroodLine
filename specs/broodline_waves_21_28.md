# Broodline — Campaign Waves 21–28

*Content, chapter four authored*

> **CURRENT — content, not design.** Schema and conventions in
> `broodline_waves_01_12.md` §1. This document is chapter 4.

**Two things this chapter found.** The four-species worst case is reachable at wave 27, not chapter 6 as chapter 3 assumed — §5. And the Wave Defeat screen as specified is not sufficient for Drift, in a way that would make the game look like it is lying to the player — §6.

---

## 1. Chapter 4 — waves 21 to 28

**Two lanes throughout. Integrity 6. Introduces Drift. Milestone 6 at wave 25.**

Chapter 4 is the first chapter with no structural change in it — no new lane count, no new geometry class. One new raider, and seven waves of the counter system being asked harder questions. That is what a chapter looks like once the teaching is done, and chapters 5 and 7 should look the same.

|---|---|
| Lanes | 2 · Fork |
| Integrity | 6 |

**One Drift, one lane, and the wave runs at 58% of budget.** Drift ignores lane geometry entirely — it flies straight, over the pockets, over the wall, over whatever the player has arranged. A player without a Reach carrier in that lane watches it cross untouched.

**The player almost certainly has Reach.** Hollow is Founder 1 and carries Reach and Pierce. So this is not a designed loss; it is the moment the player finds out what one of their oldest creature's traits was for. If they have spliced Reach out of their line — possible by wave 22 — they lose, and §6 is about making that legible.

**Drift is associated with a lane for targeting even though it ignores lane geometry for movement.** It flies over lane A's ground; a Reach carrier assigned to lane A can hit it. This distinction matters at wave 24 and it should be visible on the pre-wave screen.

**Two Drifts, simultaneously, one per lane, on lanes that do not converge.** Reach I covers one. The other crosses untouched and costs 2 integrity.

**The rest of the wave is deliberately routine** — a Lash and a stream of Skirmishers the player has handled twenty times — so their attention is where the lesson is. This is chapter 4's fusing wave, the same job wave 12 did for Chill and wave 18 for Cinder, and the pattern is now firm enough to state: **introduce a counter, then two waves later, show its tier.**

**Two answers exist and both are correct.** Fuse to Reach II, or field a second Reach carrier. The second is cheaper and the first is better, and a player working that out for themselves is the game teaching its own economy.

**The first wave in the game where the four counters do not cluster.** Drift wants Reach on Hollow, Courser wants Chill on Pale, Lash wants Taunt on Vetch, Skirmisher wants Splash on Ember. Four raider types, four different species, **four of five deployment slots committed before the player has made a single choice.**

Chapter 3 assumed this could not happen before chapter 6. It was wrong — the four counters that scatter are all available by wave 22, and letting the case arrive by accident in chapter 6 would have been worse than authoring it here on purpose. §5 covers why here is the right place.

**One free slot.** It goes to a second Reach carrier for the two Drifts, or to Screen protecting the Hollow, or to a Regrow carrier if the player has been losing creatures. There is a right answer for a given roster and no right answer in general, which is what a wave at the deployment cap should feel like.

## 3. Why Drift's wave runs at 58%

Lower than Brood's introduction at 90% and lower than the lane change at 68%. That is not inconsistency; it follows from what the cost number means.

> **A raider's cost prices the answered case.**

Ninety points of Brood is one body if the player holds Cinder and thirteen if they do not. Sixty-five points of Drift is a 130 HP body if the player holds Reach and **a guaranteed arrival** if they do not — there is no partial outcome, no chipping it down, no trading integrity for time. Untargetable is the widest gap between answered and unanswered cost in the roster.

**So a wave introducing a raider must spend less the harder its lock is.** The budget assumes the counter is held and correctly deployed, and an introduction wave is precisely the wave where that assumption is least safe.

Ordering the eight raiders by that gap gives the discount for each introduction wave:

| Lock | Raiders | Introduction wave should run at |
|---|---|---|
| **Untargetable** | Drift, Delver | ~55–60% |
| **Multiplying** | Brood | ~85–90% |
| **Immune to normal damage** | Breaker, Bulwark | ~60–65% |
| **Too fast** | Courser | ~70% |
| **Soft** | Skirmisher, Lash | 90%+ |

Chapter 5 introduces Bulwark and chapter 6 Delver; both should be authored against this rather than against a general instinct that new things need room.

## 5. The four-species case arrives at wave 27

Chapter 3 recorded that the worst case of the deployment audit "cannot occur before chapter 6," reasoning that it needed Bulwark, Delver or Breaker. **That was wrong.**

The four counters that scatter across four species are **Reach, Chill, Taunt and Splash** — Hollow, Pale, Vetch, Ember. All four raiders that want them are available from wave 22, the moment Drift arrives.

**Authoring it deliberately at wave 27 is better than discovering it at wave 38.** The audit at `broodline_combat_numbers.md` §8.1 concluded that the worst case is "tight but legal, and tight in the right way" — it is the wave that forces a player to own all six species rather than the two efficient ones. That argument is stronger at wave 27, when the roster is still shallow, than at wave 38, when a player has probably solved it without noticing.

**It should stay rare.** One wave in chapter 4, and it should not recur before chapter 6. A campaign that reaches the deployment cap routinely has stopped offering choices.

## 6. Wave Defeat is not sufficient for Drift

Bible §4.11 specifies that the Wave Defeat screen names the raider that broke through and the trait that would have answered it. That is right for Courser at wave 6, where the player does not have Chill and the screen hands them a Pale.

**It is wrong for Drift**, and authoring waves 22 and 24 is what makes it obvious.

A player loses wave 24 to a second Drift. The screen says: *Drift — answered by Reach.* The player has Reach. They have had Reach since their first Founder. **The screen has told them something they know, and implied they lack something they have.**

Three distinct failures produce the same defeat, and the screen currently cannot distinguish them:

| What happened | What the screen must say |
|---|---|
| No Reach carrier in the deployment | *"No creature you deployed carries Reach."* |
| Reach I, two Drifts in two lanes | *"Your Reach covers one lane. Two Drifts arrived in two."* |
| Reach carrier assigned to the wrong lane | *"Your Reach carrier covered lane A. The Drift came down lane B."* |

**The distinction is between access, coverage and placement**, and it maps exactly onto the three things the combat model asks a player to get right. A screen that collapses all three into "you needed Reach" is not a teaching surface; it is a scoreboard.

**This is a change to bible §4.11 and it is not cosmetic.** The Wave Defeat screen is the single surface carrying the weight of the entire counter model — bible §4.4 states that a wrong-trait defence does not work, which means losing has to be legible or the design reads as arbitrary. Getting it right for Drift gets it right for Delver, Bulwark and Breaker too.

**It also raises the pre-wave screen's job.** `broodline_raider_roster.md` §5 requires the composition panel to mark raiders the current deployment cannot handle. That check must run on coverage and lane assignment, not merely on whether the trait is present anywhere in the five.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Is wave 22 at 58% too generous?** It is the lowest-spend wave since the tutorial and Drift is a raider the player almost certainly can answer. The argument for it is that a player who cannot answer it has no partial outcome available, and that case deserves the room.
2. **Wave 27's four-species requirement is checkable and untested.** It is the first wave that can be failed by roster composition rather than by play, and it is the exact case the deployment cap was audited against. It should be instrumented from the first playable build alongside the Litter and Skirmisher questions.
3. **The Shelf's staggered arrival may be too subtle.** Two Coursers entering together and arriving eleven seconds apart is a good idea that the player may simply not perceive. It may need a pre-wave note, or it may need the gap widened past six tiles.
4. **Nothing in chapters 1–4 tests the four *utility* traits.** Carapace, Litter, Regrow and Screen have not been required by a single authored wave. That is correct — they are choices rather than requirements — but it means forty waves could pass before a player discovers whether they are worth a slot, which is the design's largest open risk going untested for most of the campaign.
