# Broodline — Campaign Waves 53–60

*Content, chapter eight authored. The campaign is complete.*

> **CURRENT — content, not design.** Schema and conventions in
> `broodline_waves_01_12.md` §1. This document is chapter 8, the last.

**A finding nobody had noticed: the Sunder cannot share a wave with Breaker or Bulwark.** It needs Pierce and Sprint, and bible §4.5 forbids two raiders answered by the same trait. That constraint decides what wave 60 can contain — §5.

---

## 1. A different authoring problem

Chapter 7 reached the composition ceiling at wave 51. A legal three-lane wave cannot exceed about 1,665 points, and chapter 8 has the same eight raiders, the same four-type cap and the same coverage tiers. **There is nothing left to add.**

So chapter 8 is not a ramp. It is **eight variations on a fixed set**, each one a different question about the same pieces:

| Dial | |
|---|---|
| **Simultaneity** | Everything at once, or in blocks |
| **Ordering** | What the player sees before they commit |
| **Lane distribution** | Symmetric, or concentrated |
| **Composition shape** | Which four of eight, and which counters that forces |
| **The Sunder** | The only genuinely new thing, at wave 60 |

**Budget is not a constraint here at all.** Every wave runs at 47–66% of formula and none could legally go higher. The numbers are recorded at §2 for completeness and they mean nothing.

**Integrity is 10 throughout.** More gets through before the pool empties, which cuts both ways — it makes the chapter survivable and it makes individual leaks cheap. That is the correct trade for a chapter where the player should be losing to composition rather than to a single mistake.

**Terrain is Delta throughout.** Three parallel lanes, four pockets, no merge point. Chapter 7 introduced it at wave 49 and chapter 8 never leaves it.

## 2. Chapter 8 — waves 53 to 60

**Three lanes, Delta. Integrity 10. Milestones 11 at wave 56 and 12 at wave 60.**

### Wave 53 — all at once

| | |
|---|---|
| Composition | **3 Breakers · 3 Bulwarks · 3 Brood · 50 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | **Everything from t=3 to t=6.** Breakers and Bulwarks simultaneous, one of each per lane. Brood at t=5. Skirmishers from t=3 at 1.0s |
| Budget | 1,565 of 2,306. 68% |
| Question | Can you triage? |
| First clear | 1,720 shards · 3 tier-I samples |

**This is wave 51's composition, delivered simultaneously.** Identical contents, and the reason chapter 8 opens with it is to make the chapter's method legible in its first wave: nothing new, and it is harder.

Wave 51 staggered its Breakers, Bulwarks and Broods across eleven seconds. This gives the player three seconds. Pierce and Sprint are both wanted immediately, on three lanes each, with no time to reposition into anything.

### Wave 54 — sequential

| | |
|---|---|
| Composition | **3 Breakers · 3 Delvers · 3 Drifts · 60 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | **Four blocks.** Breakers t=3. Delvers t=18. Drifts t=33. Skirmishers from t=45, 1.2s |
| Budget | 1,455 of 2,398. 61% |
| Question | Can you hold four separate problems in sequence? |
| First clear | 1,750 shards · 3 tier-I samples |

**The opposite of wave 53, and a different kind of hard.** Nothing overlaps, so no single moment demands two counters — but the wave runs ninety seconds and every phase wants a different creature to be the one that matters.

**The trap is regeneration.** A creature that falls in the Breaker phase is unavailable for the Drift phase, and bible §4.11 puts regeneration at 20–60 minutes. Wave 54 is the wave that makes Regrow look interesting, in the same way wave 50 makes Screen look interesting.

### Wave 55 — asymmetric

| | |
|---|---|
| Composition | **3 Breakers · 3 Bulwarks · 3 Brood · 60 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | **Lane B: all three Breakers and all three Bulwarks**, t=3 to t=10. **Lanes A and C: Brood at t=5, Skirmishers from t=4 at 1.3s** |
| Budget | 1,665 of 2,494. 67% — **the exact ceiling** |
| Question | Will you over-commit? |
| First clear | 1,780 shards · 2 tier-I samples · 1 Gen-1 creature |

**Everything heavy in one lane and harassment in the other two.** Pierce III and Sprint III on two creatures handle lane B; the question is what the other three are doing while the outer lanes fill with Brood splits.

**A player who commits four creatures to lane B wins it and loses the wave.** That is the whole point, and it is a lesson no earlier wave teaches — every previous three-lane wave has been roughly symmetric, so nothing has punished over-focus.

**This is the heaviest wave in the campaign at 1,665**, the arithmetic maximum of a legal composition. Wave 56 ties it and nothing exceeds it.

### Wave 56 — mirror

| | |
|---|---|
| Composition | **3 Breakers · 3 Bulwarks · 3 Brood · 60 Skirmishers** — one of each per lane |
| Timeline | **Identical in all three lanes.** Breaker t=3, Bulwark t=5, Brood t=9, Skirmishers from t=4 at 1.3s |
| Budget | 1,665 of 2,594. 64% — ties the ceiling |
| Question | Does your coverage reach three lanes? |
| First clear | 1,900 shards · 3 tier-I samples · **Core milestone 11** |

**Three identical lanes, perfectly symmetric.** The purest test of coverage tiers in the game: whatever answers one lane must answer all three, and there is no geometry, no timing and no distribution to exploit.

**It wants Pierce III, Sprint III and Cinder III simultaneously.** That is twenty-seven tier-I sample equivalents across three creatures, and a generation-5 line for each. **A player who has been fusing since wave 12 walks it. A player who has been collecting creatures instead does not.**

Same composition as wave 55, redistributed. Two waves, identical contents, opposite questions.

### Wave 57 — late reveal

| | |
|---|---|
| Composition | **3 Breakers · 3 Bulwarks · 3 Brood · 50 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | **t=0–20: Skirmishers only**, one per second, thin. **t=20: everything else at once**, one Breaker and one Bulwark per lane, Brood at t=24 |
| Budget | 1,565 of 2,698. 58% |
| Question | Where did you commit before you knew? |
| First clear | 1,950 shards · 3 tier-I samples |

**Twenty seconds that look like a Skirmisher wave.** Placement happens before the wave starts and creatures do not move except by Skittish, so the player has committed by the time the real wave arrives.

**The pre-wave composition panel shows what is coming**, per `broodline_raider_roster.md` §5, so this is not a trick. It is a test of whether the player read it. A wave that punished a player for information they were never given would be the design breaking its own rule; a wave that punishes them for not reading a panel that was open in front of them is fair.

### Wave 58 — the rehearsal

| | |
|---|---|
| Composition | **3 Breakers · 3 Bulwarks · 3 Delvers · 50 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | Breakers t=3, one per lane. Bulwarks t=6, one per lane. Delvers t=10, one per lane. Skirmishers from t=4 at 1.4s |
| Budget | 1,520 of 2,806. 54% |
| Question | Pierce and Sprint at once, plus Burrow. **Four species** |
| First clear | 2,000 shards · 2 tier-I samples · 1 Gen-1 creature |

**The second and last deliberate four-species wave**, after wave 27. Pierce on Hollow, Sprint on Skitter, Burrow on Loam, Splash on Ember — four of five slots committed before the player chooses anything.

**It is the Sunder rehearsal.** Wave 60 asks for Pierce and Sprint against a single body; this asks for them against six bodies across three lanes, which is the same demand on the roster arriving in a shape the player already understands. Meeting the requirement twice, two waves apart, is what stops the capstone reading as an ambush.

### Wave 59 — everything untouchable

| | |
|---|---|
| Composition | **3 Breakers · 3 Delvers · 3 Drifts · 60 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | Skirmishers from t=3 at 1.2s. Breakers t=4, one per lane. Delvers t=8, one per lane. Drifts t=13, one per lane |
| Budget | 1,455 of 2,918. 50% |
| Question | Three lanes of sky, three of ground, three of armour |
| First clear | 2,050 shards · 3 tier-I samples |

**The Hollow squeeze at maximum.** Breaker wants Pierce, Drift wants Reach, and both are Hollow traits — one creature with both at tier III, or two Hollows out of five slots. Then Burrow III on a Loam and Splash on an Ember, and the deployment is full.

Wave 42 put Drift and Delver together for the first time. This adds Breaker to it, which is the composition wave 42 was rehearsing.

### Wave 60 — the Sunder

| | |
|---|---|
| Composition | **1 Sunder · 3 Coursers · 3 Delvers · 60 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | Skirmishers from t=3 at 1.2s. Delvers t=6, one per lane. Coursers t=10, one per lane. **Sunder t=4, lane B** |
| Budget | 1,380 of 3,035. 45% |
| Question | Did you breed broadly? |
| First clear | 3,000 shards · 6 tier-I samples · **Core milestone 12** · **campaign complete** |

**One Sunder, a Hauler at 1.5× scale wearing both Plate and Shield, answered by Pierce and Sprint together.** Sixty seconds crossing lane B, and neither counter alone touches it. **Six integrity of a pool of 10** — it is the single most expensive body in the game to let through.

**Five counters, five species, five slots.** Pierce on Hollow and Sprint on Skitter for the Sunder, Chill on Pale for the Coursers, Burrow on Loam for the Delvers, Splash on Ember for the Skirmishers. **Every deployment slot is committed before the player makes a single choice**, and only Vetch is unused.

That is beyond the four-species worst case the audit at `broodline_combat_numbers.md` §8.1 blessed as "tight but legal." It is deliberate, it happens exactly once, and §6 explains why it is the right ending.

**And it is the smallest-spend wave in the chapter at 1,380.** The capstone of a sixty-wave campaign is 45% of its budget and one of the hardest waves in it, which is the clearest possible statement that this game was never about how much arrives.

## 4. The chapter teaches nothing

Chapter 3 had five teaching waves, chapter 5 two, chapter 7 four. **Chapter 8 has none.**

Every wave is a question about pieces the player already has. No new raider until the last one, no new terrain, no new counter, no new coverage tier. That is what a final chapter should be, and it is the payoff for the introduce-then-show-the-tier rhythm the previous seven chapters kept: by wave 53 the player knows all eight raiders and all eight counters, and the only thing left to ask is whether they can use them.

---

## 5. The Sunder cannot share a wave with Breaker or Bulwark

**Bible §4.5: never two raiders answered by the same trait.** The Sunder is answered by Pierce and Sprint. Breaker is answered by Pierce and Bulwark by Sprint.

**So a wave containing the Sunder can contain neither of its parents.** Nobody had noticed, and it decides what wave 60 is.

The obvious capstone — the Sunder escorted by Breakers and Bulwarks, the two variants it combines — is illegal. It would also have been the wrong wave: three Breakers plus a Sunder is four bodies wanting Pierce, which a single Pierce III carrier handles, and the wave would have been *easier* than wave 51.

**What the constraint forces is better.** Removing Pierce and Sprint from the rest of the wave means the other three types must be answered by three other traits, which pushes the wave to five counters across five species. The rule that looked like an obstacle produced the only wave in the campaign that consumes the entire deployment.

**It also generalises.** Any future dual-lock raider excludes both its parents from its own waves, which sharply limits what can be built around one — a further argument for `broodline_raider_roster.md` §7's rule that exactly one ever exists.

## 6. Five species is the right ending

The deployment audit blessed four species as "tight but legal, and tight in the right way — it is the wave that forces a player to own all six species rather than the two efficient ones." Wave 60 goes one further and commits all five slots.

**Four reasons that is correct here and nowhere else.**

**Core tier 12 sits behind it.** The last rung of the game's longest progression track, and `broodline_economy_model.md` §7 rests the entire anti-whale structure on the campaign being unpurchasable. A capstone beatable by a narrow roster would make that rung meaningless.

**It is checkable before the wave starts.** The pre-wave panel names all four raider types and the counters each needs, and marks the ones the deployment cannot handle. A player who arrives without a Pale sees it before they commit, not after.

**Failure costs nothing.** Regeneration timers, a free retry, no attempt limit. A player who fails wave 60 fifteen times has lost fifteen minutes and learned what to breed.

**And it is the one place the design should say what it means.** Sixty waves have argued that breadth beats depth and that a bred roster beats a bought one. The last wave states it: five species, five counters, five slots, nothing spare.

## 7. After wave 60

Per `broodline_campaign_structure.md` §10: **Calibration** absorbs shards past Core tier 12, **seasonal chapters** continue from wave 61 with Core capped at 12, and **the scarcity ratchet keeps running** — each seasonal chapter completion still grants the player's rarest species.

**Seasonal waves inherit chapter 8's problem and its method.** They have the same eight raiders, the same cap, the same ceiling. They cannot escalate by composition either, and they must not introduce a second dual-lock raider. **What they can do is what chapter 8 does — ask new questions with the same pieces** — and a seasonal chapter should be authored as eight shapes rather than eight difficulty steps.

**A seasonal chapter that adds a ninth raider is adding a new body**, per `broodline_raider_roster.md` §10, because a new lock needs a new silhouette. That is a real production cost and it should be a deliberate season-scale decision, not the default answer to running out of escalation.

## 8. The campaign is complete

Sixty waves, eight chapters, twelve Core milestones, eight raiders introduced one at a time, six species guaranteed inside the first week, one designed loss at wave 6 and one capstone at wave 60.

**Authoring it found ten things wrong with the specs it was authored against.** Three soft locks that were not locks, a budget formula that could not fund its own second wave and then stopped binding entirely, a raider introduced in two different places, a session boundary in the wrong spot, a loss screen that collapsed three failures into one, a composition ceiling nobody had calculated, and a legality constraint on the capstone that decided what the capstone is.

**None of those would have been found by reading the specs.** That is the argument for authoring content early, and it is the argument for authoring the seasonal chapters before the season is scheduled rather than after.

## 9. Open questions

1. **Does wave 53 land as harder than wave 51?** The whole chapter's method rests on identical composition delivered simultaneously being a different problem. If it does not land, chapter 8 has no escalation at all.
2. **Wave 54's regeneration trap is untested and it is the only wave that tests Regrow.** Like wave 50 and Screen, if it does not work there it does not work anywhere.
3. **Is six integrity right for the Sunder?** It is the highest single cost in the game against a pool of 10, so one leak plus two Coursers ends the wave. That may be exactly right for a capstone or it may make wave 60 a coin flip on one body.
4. **Wave 60 has no free slot.** Every other wave in the campaign leaves at least one, and the difference between "tight" and "forced" is the difference between a good capstone and a frustrating one. It should be the second thing instrumented after wave 50.
5. **Sixty Skirmishers plus three Broods is close to a hundred entities**, flagged in chapters 6 and 7 and recurring in five of chapter 8's waves. It is a performance question that needs answering before the first playable build, not after.
