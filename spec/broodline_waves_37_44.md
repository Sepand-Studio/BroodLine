## 1. Chapter 6 — waves 37 to 44

**Two lanes rising to three. Integrity 8. Introduces Delver. Milestone 8 at wave 41.**

Two structural changes, separated the way chapter 3 separated Brood from the second lane. **Delver arrives at wave 38 on two lanes.** **The third lane arrives at wave 40 with nothing new in it.**

That pushes the Burrow coverage lesson to wave 41, one wave later than the established two-wave rhythm. It lands better there — three Delvers across three lanes is a harder version of the same question than two across two would have been.

**57% is the same discount Drift took at wave 22**, and for the same reason — untargetable has no partial outcome. See §5 for why Delver only became untargetable during this chapter.

## 4. Delver and Drift together

Wave 42 is worth calling out as the composition the whole counter system was built to produce.

Two raiders, neither touchable by anything in the pockets, arriving from opposite directions. The player's five creatures have to include a Reach carrier for the sky and a Burrow carrier for the ground, at coverage tiers matching three lanes each, and still handle everything walking down the middle.

**It is the first wave where fusing is not optional.** Every earlier coverage lesson could be answered by a second carrier instead — two Hollows for two Drifts, two Pales for two Coursers. Wave 42 wants three lanes of sky and three lanes of ground simultaneously, and five slots cannot hold six carriers.

**That makes it the first genuine test of whether the sample economy supplies coverage fast enough.** A player at wave 42 is roughly eight weeks in. If they cannot field Reach II and Burrow II by then, the sample drop rates at `broodline_sample_economy.md` §3 are too low, and this is the wave that will show it.

## 5. Delver was the third soft lock

`broodline_waves_29_36.md` §5 flagged Delver as the last raider not re-checked after Bulwark's shield turned out not to be a lock. It is not one either.

**As originally specified**, Delver submerged for the first 70% of the lane and surfaced at tile 17, level with the rear pockets. That leaves seven tiles at 0.6 tiles per second — **11.7 seconds targetable**, against 200 HP. Five creatures averaging around 19 DPS deal over a thousand damage in that window. It dies without Burrow, comfortably, every time.

> **Revised: Delver travels submerged for the entire lane and surfaces at the Ark.**
> **Burrow forces it up at the lane's midpoint — one Delver at tier I, two at II, three at III.**

Untargetable end to end, exactly like Drift, and answered the same way. The difference between them stays meaningful: Drift is fast and overhead, Delver is slow and underfoot, and the disturbance tracking up the lane makes Delver an anticipation threat where Drift is an immediate one.

**Tier III no longer surfaces Delvers at spawn.** Coverage scales by count alone, like Pierce, Sprint, Chill, Taunt and Splash. Only Cinder and Reach scale on depth, and adding a third exception would make the ladder harder to teach than it needs to be.

### 5.1 Why three of eight were soft

Breaker, Bulwark and Delver. All three failed the same way, and the pattern is worth holding.

> **A lock is only a lock if it is written as a rule about damage or targeting. A description of the raider's condition is not a rule.**

*Armoured*, *shielded* and *submerged* are conditions. Each was specified with a plausible number attached — a 5-damage cap, a 25-per-hit shield, a 70% submersion — and in all three cases the arithmetic let ordinary damage through the side.

The five that were sound were written as rules from the start. Courser is a rule about time. Brood is a rule about what happens on death. Drift is a rule about what can be targeted. Skirmisher and Lash are rules about volume and target selection, and both are soft *by design*.

**All eight now pass.** No further re-checks are outstanding, and any seasonal raider should be written against this test before its stats are set.

## 6. The budget formula stops binding at three lanes

Every three-lane wave in this chapter runs at 60–90% of budget, and the gap does not close as the chapter progresses. That is not authoring restraint. **It is not possible to spend the budget legally.**

The formula multiplies by lane count, so wave 44 at three lanes gets 1,621. Spending it requires either more raider types or more of each, and both are capped:

- **Four raider types maximum**, per bible §4.5
- **Coverage tiers top out at three targets**, so more than three of a raider type is a difficulty cliff rather than an escalation — Burrow III forces up three Delvers and the fourth is simply unanswerable
- **Skirmishers are the only unbounded filler**, and sixty of them is 600 points of a 1,621 budget

Practical ceiling for a legal three-lane wave: **three each of three raider types, plus Skirmisher filler — around 1,400 to 1,500.** Wave 44 is at 1,455 and is close to the maximum legal composition, at 90% of its budget.

**So from wave 40 the four-type cap is the binding constraint and the budget is a sanity check.** That is a finding rather than a failure — the formula did its job through forty waves and two lane counts — but it has two consequences.

**Chapters 7 and 8 should be authored against the type cap**, with budget checked afterward rather than targeted. Expect three-lane waves to sit at 80–95% and stop worrying about it.

**Wave 60's budget cannot be read as a wave.** `broodline_combat_numbers.md` §8 describes it as "roughly twenty Breakers, or a mixed wave of comparable weight." **Twenty Breakers is not a legal wave** — it is more than four types' worth of one type, on three lanes, against a Pierce tier that answers three. The realistic wave 60 is three or four of each of four types, around 1,500 to 1,800, against a budget near 3,000. That should be corrected before the Sunder is authored, since it changes what a capstone wave can contain.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Does the disturbance tell make Delver too easy to plan around?** A player who can see exactly where an untargetable threat is may simply pre-place the Burrow carrier and never feel the tension. Hiding it entirely is worse, but a delayed or intermittent tell might be better than a continuous one.
2. **Wave 42 is the sample-economy test and it arrives at week eight.** If coverage cannot be fused by then, either the drop rates move or the wave does. It should be instrumented specifically.
3. **Sixty Skirmishers at wave 44 may be a performance problem before it is a design one.** Sixty bodies plus three Broods becoming thirty-nine is close to a hundred entities on a phone.
4. **The Weir's short lanes may interact badly with Bulwark.** Fifty seconds instead of sixty is a 17% cut to the time available for the slowest raider in the game, and Bulwark is the raider whose whole design is about being given time.
5. **Still nothing has required a utility trait.** Forty-four waves. Wave 42's slot pressure is the closest — a player with Screen protecting the Hollow may survive it more easily — but no wave has demanded one, and the design's largest open risk remains untested by the campaign.
