# Broodline — Campaign Waves 1–12

*Content, chapters one and two authored*

> **CURRENT — content, not design.** `broodline_campaign_structure.md` owns the
> chapter table, milestone waves and raider order. This document is the authored
> instance of chapters 1 and 2, and the schema every remaining wave follows.

**Authoring these twelve found four things wrong.** They are at §5 and three of them change other documents.

---

## 1. The schema

Every wave carries the same eight fields. Anything not stated here is inherited from the chapter.

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Lanes** | 1–3, authored per wave, not derived from the Ark's region |
| **Integrity** | The Ark's pool for this wave. Authored, 2 early to 10 late |
| **Composition** | Raider types and counts. Never two answered by the same trait; never more than four types |
| **Timeline** | When each group enters, in seconds from wave start |
| **Budget** | Spend against the formula, and any authored deviation |
| **Teaches** | The one thing this wave is for. If a wave teaches nothing new it is a difficulty step, and that is a legitimate answer |
| **Expected roster** | What the player plausibly has. Waves are tuned against this, never gated on it |
| **First clear** | Shards, samples, creature, and any milestone or guarantee |

**Raider costs**, revised — see §5.1:

| Raider | Cost |
|---|---|
| Skirmisher | **10** each |
| Lash | 60 |
| Courser | 85 |
| Brood | 90 |
| Delver | 75 |
| Drift | 65 |
| Bulwark | 120 |
| Breaker | 145 |

## 2. Chapter 1 — waves 1 to 5

**One lane. Integrity 2. Introduces Skirmisher and Lash. Milestone 1 at wave 3.**

Chapter 1 spans two sessions. **Waves 1 and 2 are session one**; waves 3 to 5 begin session two. That split is not cosmetic — see §5.3.

### Wave 1

| | |
|---|---|
| Lanes | 1 · Defile layout, 6 pockets |
| Integrity | 2 |
| Composition | **6 Skirmishers** |
| Timeline | One every 2.0s from t=3 |
| Budget | 60 of 100. Deliberately under |
| Teaches | Creatures fire on their own. You place, they fight |
| Expected roster | 2 — the tutorial Vetch and Ember |
| First clear | 150 shards · 2 tier-I samples |

**Six, at two seconds apart, is slower than the Skirmisher's designed pattern.** The real interval is 1.5s and the real unit is eight. Wave 1 is not testing the player; it is showing them that placement produces a result. The first genuine Skirmisher wave is wave 3.

Bible §9.2 puts this wave twenty seconds into the app with one instruction. The lane is a Defile — six pockets for two creatures — so no placement is wrong.

### Wave 2

| | |
|---|---|
| Lanes | 1 · Defile |
| Integrity | 2 |
| Composition | **8 Skirmishers · 1 Lash** |
| Timeline | Skirmishers from t=3, 1.5s apart. Lash at t=12 |
| Budget | 140 of 104. **135% — authored over** |
| Teaches | Something shoots back, and it reaches past the front line |
| Expected roster | 3 — Vetch, Ember, Hollow |
| First clear | 165 shards · 2 tier-I samples |

**Lash arrives here, not at wave 4.** Bible §9.2 beat 5 is "Skirmishers and one Lash, three creatures deployed," and beat 5 is wave 2. The chapter table said wave 4 and was wrong — see §5.2.

**Nobody is told about counters.** The Vetch holds the Lash because Taunt is on it, and the player experiences that as the big one standing in front. Bible §9.7 keeps the word "counter" out of session one entirely.

**The Lash enters at t=12, after the Skirmishers are engaged.** Arriving into a busy lane is what makes it register as a separate thing rather than as more of the same.

### Wave 3

| | |
|---|---|
| Lanes | 1 · Defile |
| Integrity | 2 |
| Composition | **10 Skirmishers · 1 Lash** |
| Timeline | Skirmishers from t=2, 1.5s apart. Lash at t=10 |

| Teaches | Nothing new. First real difficulty step |
| Expected roster | 3 — Hollow, Cinderplate, Vetch (Founder 2) |

**The roster changed shape between waves 2 and 3 and the player did not lose ground.** The tutorial splice consumed the Vetch and the Ember; Founder 2 arrives before this wave to keep the deployment at three. §5.3 explains why that matters more than it looks.

**Ten Skirmishers at 1.5s is the first wave that punishes a single-target defence.** Retarget delay is 0.4s, so bodies get through the gaps. Cinderplate carries Splash from the guided tutorial splice, which is the answer, and the player will not know that is what happened.

**The first wave that shows a coverage tier doing something.** One Vetch with Taunt I pins one Lash; the other picks the furthest target, which will be the Hollow. Lash is a soft lock — answerable by enough output — so this is survivable and instructive rather than a wall.

**The lane narrows to a Basin.** Four pockets rather than six, so the player has to decide where the Hollow stands for the first time.

**Integrity rises to 3 here**, which is the first time a leak is survivable. That is deliberate placement: the chapter ends by teaching that not everything has to be stopped, one wave before a chapter that opens by teaching that some things cannot be.

## 3. Chapter 2 — waves 6 to 12

**One lane. Integrity 2 rising to 4. Introduces Courser. Milestones 2 at wave 8 and 3 at wave 12.**

### Wave 6 — the designed loss

| | |
|---|---|
| Lanes | 1 · Defile |
| Integrity | **2** |
| Composition | **1 Courser.** Nothing else |
| Timeline | t=3 |
| Budget | 85 of 122. **Deliberately under** |
| Teaches | **The counter system exists** |
| Expected roster | 5, none carrying Chill |
| First clear | On defeat: **Pale granted**, free retry. On clear: same grant |

**One Courser, alone, down an empty lane.**

Every earlier draft of this wave had a screen of Skirmishers around it. That would have been a mistake. With integrity at 2, a leaked Skirmisher also costs the wave, and the player would finish uncertain whether the thing that killed them was the fast one or the small ones. **The lesson only lands if there is exactly one possible answer to "what happened."**

An empty lane with a single animal sprinting down it is also the most dramatic image in the first hour, and it costs nothing to author.

- Courser ignores Taunt, so the Vetch does not save them
- Fifteen seconds to cross, so five creatures cannot focus it down
- Integrity 2, so one arrival ends it
- **Wave Defeat names Courser and names Chill**, per bible §4.11
- **The Wave Defeat screen grants a Pale**, framed as a Warden resupply
- Free retry, immediately

The player loses, is told exactly why, is handed the answer, and wins inside two minutes.

**If they somehow win, the Pale grant fires anyway** and the beat is degraded rather than broken. Tune so this does not happen.

|---|---|

| Integrity | 3 |
| Composition | **1 Lash · 6 Skirmishers** |

**A deliberately easy wave straight after the designed loss.** The player just lost for the first time and was handed a creature; the next thing that happens should be a clean win with the new animal on the board. Making wave 7 hard would convert a taught lesson into a difficulty spike.

**The first wave that requires the player to hold three counters at once.** Splash, Taunt and Chill, across a five-creature deployment. It is the shape of every later wave in miniature, and it arrives while the roster is small enough that the constraint is felt.

| | |

This is the first wave in the game where fusing a sample is the difference, and it is placed at a milestone so the player has a reason to be paying attention.

## 3.1 Budget against spend

| Wave | Budget | Spend | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 60 | 60% — tutorial, under |
| 2 | 104 | 140 | 135% |
| 3 | 108 | 160 | 148% |
| 4 | 112 | 180 | 160% |
| 5 | 117 | 240 | 205% |
| **6** | 122 | 85 | **70% — the designed loss, under** |
| 7 | 127 | 120 | 95% |
| 8 | 132 | 125 | 95% |
| 9 | 137 | 195 | 142% |
| 10 | 142 | 180 | 126% |
| 11 | 148 | 205 | 138% |
| 12 | 154 | 250 | 162% — milestone |

**Chapters 1 and 2 run consistently over budget, and the two waves that run under are the two that matter most.** That is not the formula failing a second time — it is the formula being wrong about *where it starts applying*. Waves 1 to 5 are hand-set tutorial content and should be excluded from it entirely. Waves 7 and 8 sit at 95%, which is the first evidence the base of 100 is right once the tutorial is behind.

**Waves 9 to 12 at 126–162% are the real signal to watch.** If chapter 3 also needs 140% to feel right, the growth rate rather than the base is wrong, and 1.04 should rise to about 1.05 before forty-eight more waves are authored against it.

## 4. What the twelve waves do together

| Waves | |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Session one. Placement, then something that fights back |
| 3–5 | Volume, then coverage, then a climax. All soft locks |
| **6** | **The counter system, taught by losing** |
| 7–8 | Recovery, then payoff |
| 9 | Three counters at once |
| 10–12 | Volume, order, simultaneity — the three dials the rest of the campaign uses |

**No hard lock appears before wave 6, and only one appears in the first twelve waves.** Skirmisher and Lash are the two softest locks in the game and they carry chapter 1 alone. That is deliberate: the player spends five waves learning that placement matters before the game tells them that placement is not enough.

**Every wave in chapter 2 is winnable with the roster the guaranteed path provides.** Nothing here requires a splice outcome, a mutation, or a specific roll.

## 5. Four corrections

Authoring twelve waves against the specs found four errors. Three change other documents.

### 5.1 The budget formula's base is too low

`broodline_combat_numbers.md` §8 gives **Budget(w) = 60 × 1.04^(w−1) × L**, anchored so wave 1 is one Skirmisher unit of eight.

**At that base, the formula funds almost nothing for twenty waves.** Wave 2 gets 62 and a Lash costs 60. Wave 4 gets 68, which is one Lash and no room for anything alongside it. Every wave in chapters 1 and 2 has to be authored far over budget, which means the formula is not doing its job.

Two changes:

- **Skirmishers cost 10 each rather than 60 for a unit of eight.** A unit-priced quantum that large has no granularity at any budget below 200. Individual pricing also makes wave 1's six Skirmishers — bible §9.2's number — cost exactly 60
- **Raise the base to 100.** Budget(w) = **100 × 1.04^(w−1) × L**. Wave 60 at three lanes becomes ~3,000, which is around twenty Breakers or a mixed wave of that weight — the same end state, reached from a base that funds the start

All other raider costs are rescaled by the same factor and are in §1.

### 5.2 Lash is introduced at wave 2, not wave 4

`broodline_campaign_structure.md` §2 puts Lash at wave 4. Its own §3 puts beat 5 — "Skirmishers and one Lash" — at wave 2. **Both cannot be true.** Beat 5 is the bible's and it wins.

### 5.3 Chapter 1 spans two sessions, and it has to

The tutorial splice at beat 6 consumes the Vetch and the Ember, taking a three-creature roster to two. Founder 2 arrives on day 1.

**If wave 3 sits in session one, the player fights it with fewer creatures than wave 2** — a difficulty spike that looks like the game punishing them for following the tutorial. Session one therefore ends after wave 2, and wave 3 opens session two with Founder 2 already in the roster.

That is a scheduling fact the FTUE and the campaign both need to state, and neither currently does.

### 5.4 Milestone waves need budget headroom

A chapter's closing wave should feel like a climax, and the formula does not allow one — wave 12 at 154 cannot hold two Coursers at 170.

**Rule: a wave carrying a Core milestone may run up to 15% over budget.** Every other authored deviation is a tutorial wave running deliberately *under*, which needs no rule.

*Waves 1–5 sit far over the formula even after §5.1, because they are hand-set tutorial content serving beats. The formula should be treated as governing from wave 6.*

## 6. Open questions

1. **Wave 4's two Lash may be too early for a coverage lesson.** It arrives before the Codex has been opened by most players, so the mechanism is felt rather than understood. That is arguably correct and arguably a wave too soon.
2. **Wave 10's 1.2-second Skirmisher interval is faster than the designed 1.5.** It is the cheapest way to make volume bite without raising the count, and it also makes wave 10 a partial test of the highest-leverage untested number in the design.
3. **Are the first-clear shard rewards too generous?** Wave 1 at 150 against a casual player's 760 a day is a fifth of a day for twenty seconds of play. Onboarding should be generous; this may be past it.
4. **Wave 6's Pale grant fires on defeat.** Granting it on the retry screen would separate the loss from the reward and might teach more cleanly, at the risk of the player quitting in between.
